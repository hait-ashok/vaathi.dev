Most modern language (and many multimodal) models are built on an architecture nicknamed the **transformer**. You don’t need the equations — you need the *job* it does.

## Attention: what to focus on

When you read “The trophy didn’t fit in the suitcase because **it** was too big,” you decide what “it” refers to. **Attention** is a learned way for the model to weigh which earlier words matter for the next prediction — a soft spotlight, not a single hard rule.

<AttentionAnim />

That “look around the context” trick is why transformers handle longer phrases better than older one-word-at-a-time designs.

## Encoder / decoder (intuition only)

Rough roles you’ll hear:

- **Encoder** — Reads and represents the input (useful for understanding / classifying a passage).
- **Decoder** — Generates the next tokens (chat and completion models lean heavily here).
- **Encoder–decoder** — Classic for translation-style “read this, write that.”

Many chat models you use are **decoder-style** generators with clever training. The names matter less than the idea: **represent context, then generate**.

See also: [Glossary — Transformer](/glossary#transformer).

## What you can ignore for now

Matrix multiplies, layer counts, and paper acronyms. They matter for builders. For Foundations, remember: transformers = **context-aware prediction engines** powered by attention.

## Takeaway

A transformer is a design that lets the model **decide what to pay attention to** in the input while predicting what comes next.
