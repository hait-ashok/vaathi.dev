Most chat use is **single-shot**: you ask, you get a reply, you’re done (or you ask again yourself). **Agentic** systems try to chase a goal by planning, calling tools, reading results, and looping until they finish — or until a limit stops them.

## Single-shot first

<SingleShotChatDiagram />

That’s enough for many beginner tasks. Your [personal AI workflow](/learn/foundations/personal-ai-workflow) sits on top: you still research, verify, and cite.

## The agentic loop

<AgenticWorkflowDiagram />

In plain words:

1. **Goal** — What should be true when we’re done?
2. **Plan** — Break the goal into steps (the system may rewrite the plan as it goes).
3. **Act** — Call tools: search, code, calendar, files, APIs.
4. **Observe** — Read what the tool returned; notice errors.
5. **Loop or finish** — Update the plan and act again, or stop when the goal looks met.

See also: [Glossary — Agent](/glossary#agent), [Agentic workflow](/glossary#agentic-workflow).

## Why beginners go slow here

- More steps means more ways to **spend money, leak data, or take a wrong action**.
- A fluent loop is not the same as a **correct** loop — you still verify outcomes.
- Prefer supervised / sandboxed agents before anything that can email, buy, or delete.

From the [tool landscape](/learn/foundations/tool-landscape): start with chat; treat agents as “powerful; start supervised.”

## Takeaway

**Single-shot** = one generation. **Agentic** = goal-driven loop with tools. Same models often power both — the *workflow shape* is what changes.
