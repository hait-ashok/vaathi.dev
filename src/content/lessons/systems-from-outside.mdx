<p class="mono-label">Scene</p>

Ash opens a chat box and asks for a meetup invite. No toolkit. No training run. Just type, wait, draft. That outside view is enough for most people — and it is how you should learn to think first.

You can understand most AI products without opening the engine bay. Think in **inputs**, **outputs**, and **services**.

## The loop

1. **You provide an input** — a prompt, a document, an image, a click.
2. **A model (somewhere) computes** — often on a company’s servers, not on your laptop.
3. **You get an output** — text, a score, a generated image, a suggested action.

Settings, system prompts, and safety filters sit around that loop. They shape what you can ask and what comes back.

## Models as services

Modern AI is usually **rented by the request**: an API or app sends your input to a hosted model and returns a result. That means:

- Quality and cost depend on which model and settings you use.
- Your data may leave your device — check privacy defaults.
- The same product can change behavior when the vendor swaps models.

## What you don’t need yet

You don’t need to know how training works, what a GPU is, or how to fine-tune. Those topics matter later if you build systems. For using AI well as a beginner, the skill is **framing inputs and judging outputs**.

## Takeaway

Treat every AI feature as a **service with a contract**: what goes in, what comes out, and what you still owe the world (checking, citing, deciding).
