When teams train or compare text models, they often want a **number** that says “how close is this output to a human reference?” Two classic metrics you’ll hear are **BLEU** and **ROUGE**.

*(People sometimes write “BLUE” by mistake — the standard name is **BLEU**.)*

## BLEU — mostly for translation

**BLEU** (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy) checks how much the model’s wording **overlaps in short word chunks** with one or more reference translations. Higher usually meant “closer to the reference” in older machine-translation benchmarks.

- **Used for:** translation-style tasks with fairly fixed “right” phrasings.
- **Intuition:** lots of the same little phrases as the reference → higher BLEU.

## ROUGE — mostly for summarization

**ROUGE** (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation) also looks at overlap with a reference, with flavors that care about **recall** (did we cover the reference’s important bits?) and sometimes longer matches.

- **Used for:** summarization and related “gisting” tasks.
- **Intuition:** did the summary catch the content the human summary cared about?

See also: [Glossary — BLEU](/glossary#bleu), [ROUGE](/glossary#rouge).

## Why they aren’t enough alone

- A summary can score well and still miss the *point*, or sound awkward.
- Creative, open-ended answers have **no single reference** — overlap metrics struggle.
- Gaming the metric (copying safe phrases) ≠ being helpful or truthful.
- Human review, task success, and safety checks still matter.

## Takeaway

**BLEU** ≈ “overlap with a translation reference.” **ROUGE** ≈ “overlap with a summary reference.” Useful lab tools — not a full grade for real-world Gen AI quality.
