When people say “AI,” they often mix three different ideas. Sorting them saves a lot of confusion later.

## Three everyday meanings

1. **Narrow tools** — A system that does one job well enough to ship: recommend a song, transcribe audio, flag spam.
2. **Machine learning (ML)** — A family of techniques that learn patterns from data instead of being fully hand-written with rules.
3. **“AI products”** — Apps that wrap models behind a friendly interface (chat, image tools, copilots). You use the product; the model is the engine.

You do **not** need to train models to use AI products well. You *do* need a clear sense of what the product is claiming to do.

## General AI is not what you’re using

Headlines about “AGI” or “human-level AI” are about a future (or speculative) capability. Today’s chatbots and image tools are powerful **narrow** systems: they generate plausible text or pixels for many prompts, but they do not understand the world the way a person does.

## Where beginners get stuck

- Treating a chat reply as a verified fact.
- Assuming every “AI” feature uses the same kind of model.
- Thinking you must learn calculus before you can start.

## Takeaway

For this path, “AI” mostly means: **software that predicts useful outputs from inputs**, often via large models served as a product. Next we’ll zoom into **generative AI** — the create-new-content branch — without treating it as the whole of AI.
