You rarely train a giant model yourself. Still, knowing the **pipeline** helps you ask better questions of vendors and of your own workflow.

## The short pipeline

1. **Data** — Examples of the job (text pairs, images + captions, chat transcripts). Quality and coverage matter more than magic. Often those examples need [human labels](/learn/foundations/how-data-is-labeled).
2. **Train or fine-tune** — The model’s internal knobs are adjusted so its outputs better match the examples. *Pre-training* builds general skill on huge data; *fine-tuning* specializes on a narrower set.
3. **Evaluate** — Humans and metrics check whether outputs are useful, safe, and on-task. Numbers help; they don’t replace judgment. (You’ll meet classic text metrics like [BLEU and ROUGE](/learn/foundations/bleu-and-rouge) later.)
4. **Deploy** — The model is wrapped as a product or API: filters, rate limits, logging, updates.

## What “training” means for you as a user

When a chat app “learns,” it might mean:

- The **base model** was trained earlier by a lab (you’re borrowing that skill).
- Your **prompt and chat history** steer this session only (usually not permanent learning).
- Optional **memory / personalization** features store preferences — check privacy settings.

You are almost always **using** a deployed model, not rebuilding it.

## Why this matters

Understanding the pipeline explains product behavior: a model can be great at fluent English and still weak on your company’s private facts (those weren’t in training). Retrieval and good prompts help bridge that gap — covered in later lessons.

## Takeaway

**Data shapes skill → training adjusts → evaluation catches gaps → deploy serves users.** Your job as a beginner is usually step “use carefully,” not step “train from scratch.”
