Ash just learned [how a model is built](/learn/foundations/how-a-model-is-built) — and now every pitch says “GPU.” Why does meetup copy care about graphics chips?

Ash has seen [transformers](/learn/foundations/transformers-beginner) lean on [attention](/glossary#attention). This lesson answers the practical “why”: **labs and clouds talk about GPUs (and other accelerators) because of the shape of the math.**

## Two kinds of good at

Think of chips as workplaces with different staffing:

- A **CPU** is great at a **few complex jobs** — branching logic, running your OS, orchestrating apps, deciding what to do next.
- A **GPU** is great at **many similar simple operations at once** — the same multiply-and-add pattern repeated across huge grids of numbers.

Neither “wins” at everything. They’re specialized.

## Why neural nets love that shape of work

Training and inference for neural nets (including transformers) spend most of their time on **matrix math**: combine many numbers, mix context, update weights. [Attention](/learn/foundations/how-transformers-work) especially creates a storm of parallel multiplies as every position looks at others.

That workload maps well to GPUs and similar **accelerators** (chips built for this pattern — you may hear TPUs or other names; same idea: parallel numerical grunt work). The point isn’t a brand. It’s **shape of compute**.

## VRAM and batching (light intuition)

- **VRAM** (GPU memory) holds the model weights and the temporary numbers for the current batch of tokens. Bigger models and longer contexts need more room.
- **Batching** means processing several examples (or several requests) together so the chip stays busy. More parallelism → better use of those many cores — until you run out of memory.

Ash doesn’t need to size hardware as a beginner. Just know: **memory and parallelism** are why “this model needs a GPU” shows up in install notes and cloud bills — including when Ash drafts an invite in a chat app.

## When CPUs still appear

CPUs don’t disappear:

- **Small models** and light demos may run fine on CPU (slower, but fine for learning).
- **Preprocessing** — cleaning text, loading files, tokenization — often starts on CPU.
- **Orchestration** — Ash’s app, the API server, queues, logging — still CPU-shaped work.
- **Laptop chat apps** may use a mix: local small models on CPU/Apple Silicon, big models on a remote GPU farm.

So: GPUs (accelerators) for the heavy model math; CPUs for running the rest of the system.

## What you can ignore for now

CUDA versions, specific card SKUs, and vendor scoreboards. Foundations care about the **job**, not the shopping list.

## Takeaway

**CPUs handle flexible control; GPUs (and accelerators) flood the chip with parallel matrix ops** — the shape of work that training and large-model inference need.
