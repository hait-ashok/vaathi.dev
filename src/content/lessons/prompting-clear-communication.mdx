Good prompting is less spellcasting and more **clear communication**. You’re briefing a fast, literal collaborator that doesn’t share your unspoken context.

## A simple template

1. **Goal** — What do you want done? (“Draft a 3-bullet summary for a non-technical reader.”)
2. **Context** — What does the model need to know? (Audience, background, source text.)
3. **Constraints** — Limits: length, tone, must/avoid, reading level.
4. **Format** — How should the answer look? (Bullets, table, email, JSON-ish structure.)

You don’t need all four every time. Start with goal + format; add context when results miss.

## Few-shot, lightly

Showing **one or two examples** of the shape you want often works better than abstract instructions. Keep examples short.

## Iterate

First answers are drafts. Refine: “Shorter.” “More skeptical.” “Only use facts from the text I pasted.” Iteration beats hunting for a mythical perfect one-liner.

## Takeaway

Prompting skill is briefing skill. Clarity beats cleverness.
